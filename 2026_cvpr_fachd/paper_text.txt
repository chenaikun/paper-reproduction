Abstract
Incremental Object Detection (IOD) enables AI systems to
continuously acquire new object classes while preserving
knowledge of previously learned ones, an ability essential
for deployment in dynamic, real-world environments. Ex-
isting IOD methods typically rely on knowledge distilla-
tion to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. However, the tight
coupling between the student model’s detection head and
backbone causes distillation gradients to conflict with new-
class supervision at the head, injecting head-specific bias
into the backbone and ultimately weakening distillation ef-
fectiveness. To address this issue, we propose a decoupled
training mechanism for the model’s backbone and classi-
fication head. Specifically, we introduce the Future-aware
Cross-head decoupled Distillation (FaCHD) method, which
utilizes two frozen complementary teachers (historical and
intermediate teachers) to decode the student’s ROI features
for cross-head distillation. This strategy implicitly allevi-
ates prediction conflicts caused by detection-head bias and
provides richer task-relevant guidance, thereby improving
distillation efficiency. To further address the detection head
bias and model recency problem, we propose a Prototype
Semantic Drift Compensation module, which recalibrates
multi-granularity prototypes of old classes, effectively cor-
recting semantic drift and enhancing the stability of the de-
tection head. Extensive experiments on two standard IOD
benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority
of the proposed method.
1. Introduction
Object detection is a fundamental task in computer vision
with critical applications in autonomous driving, robotics,
and video understanding [9, 10, 35]. Conventional object
detectors assume access to all categories and exhaustively
annotated data at once, enabling static joint training. How-

ever, real-world deployment often involves dynamic envi-
ronments where new object classes continually emerge, and
storing historical data is impractical or restricted due to
memory constraints and privacy concerns. These challenges
have motivated the study of Incremental Object Detection
(IOD) [31], which aims to continuously learn new classes
while retaining previously acquired knowledge, effectively
mitigating catastrophic forgetting in evolving, real-world
scenarios.
Following the traditional Class Incremental Learn-
ing (CIL) paradigm, existing Incremental Object De-
tection (IOD) methods can be roughly categorized
into regularization-based and replay-based approaches.
Among regularization-based methods, Knowledge Distilla-
tion (KD) [5, 8, 11, 13, 23, 29, 31, 46] plays a vital role
in mitigating catastrophic forgetting for IOD. Compared
with CIL, IOD faces unique challenges arising from the co-
occurrence of multiple task classes within the current train-
ing data: (1) foreground objects from previous tasks may
remain unlabeled in the current stage, making them prone to
misclassification as background; (2) the current-stage back-
ground may contain objects from future tasks, which may
inadvertently be treated as foreground. These factors induce
inter-class overlap and foreground-background confusion,
further exacerbating cross-task interference during knowl-
edge distillation. Although existing KD-based IOD meth-
ods impose strong regularization to prevent forgetting, they
often overlook prediction conflicts arising from distillation
in object detection models. Specifically, when distillation
is applied directly to output logits, conflicts arise between
the student model’s assigner-based ground-truth targets and
the teacher model’s predictions. Moreover, many methods
tightly couple the detection head and backbone, causing
head-specific bias (typically biased toward new classes) to
be imprinted into the backbone features, which further ag-
gravates catastrophic forgetting.
To address these challenges, we propose Future-aware
Decoupled Cross-Head Distillation (FaCHD), a novel
framework for IOD that performs feature distillation

through decoupled cross-head knowledge transfer from
both past and future detection models. Unlike conven-
tional approaches that directly enforce consistency between
intermediate feature maps or teacher-student predictions,
FaCHD explicitly conducts feature distillation under the
regularization of past and future detection heads, thereby
implicitly mitigating prediction conflicts arisen by the
detection-head bias and improving distillation efficiency.
Specifically, the intermediate features from the student’s
backbone are fed to both the past and future detection head
to produce cross-head predictions [36]. Then, prediction
concatenation and Knowledge distillation are applied be-
tween these cross-head predictions and the teachers’ out-
puts. The key innovation of FaCHD lies in its cross-head
decoding strategy: the student’s Region of Interest (ROI)
features are decoded by two teachers, each specialized in
their respective knowledge domains,i.e.,previous and cur-
rent category spaces. By decoupling the student’s detection
head from the backbone, gradients are constrained to flow
exclusively through the backbone and ROI, ensuring sta-
ble, head-independent geometric representations, so as to
address the detection haed bias and model recency prob-
lem. This strategy not only enhances training stability for
prediction mimicking, but also provides more task-oriented
information than conventional feature imitation.
To further address the detection head bias and model re-
cency problem, existing class incremental learning method
usually store prototypes/statistics for old categories, facil-
iating the joint learning with current class features. How-
ever, features of previously learned classes may become
misaligned due to semantic drift, which can reduce the ef-
ficiency of detection head learning. To this end, we pro-
pose a semantic drift compensation mechanism over multi-
granularity ROI prototypes. Specifically, we maintain a
bank of ROI prototypes at multiple granularities and esti-
mate class-specific semantic offsets by comparing feature
maps from the current (student) and historical models. Re-
calibrating these prototypes allows the model to automat-
ically correct drift without requiring annotations for old
classes. This mechanism operates primarily on the student’s
detection head, resetting task-specific decision boundaries
while preserving the underlying geometric consistency of
features. By separately addressing the tasks of backbone
geometric consistency shaping through FaCHD and head
decision boundary resetting through prototype compensa-
tion, our framework systematically and modularly mitigates
catastrophic forgetting, enhancing both stability and adapt-
ability in incremental object detection.
To summarize, our contributions are as follows:
• We propose a future-aware decoupled cross-Head distil-
lation mechanism for IOD, which performs feature dis-
tillation through decoupled cross-head knowledge trans-
fer from both past and current detection models. This
strategy not only enhances training stability for prediction
mimicking, but also provides more task-oriented informa-
tion than conventional feature imitation.
• We propose Prototype Semantic Drift Compensation
mechanism, which recalibrates multi-granularity proto-
types for old classes, solving the problem of semantic
drift and resetting task-specific decision boundary of the
detection head.
• Through extensive experiments, we validate the effective-
ness of our approach in reducing catastrophic forgetting
across two benchmark IOD datasets, achieving superior
performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.
2. Related Work
Class Incremental Learning.Existing class-incremental
learning methods can be broadly grouped into three cat-
egories: (1) regularization- and distillation-based ap-
proaches, (2) replay- and prototype-based methods, and
(3) parameter-isolation or dynamic-architecture strategies.
Our work is most closely related to the knowledge distilla-
tion–based line, which remains the dominant paradigm in
this field. LwF [19] preserves prior knowledge by align-
ing the student’s predictions with a frozen teacher, while
EWC [17] restricts updates to parameters identified as im-
portant through Fisher information. Subsequent studies fur-
ther refine distillation using adaptive regularization, and
task-aware feature or logit alignment [1, 2, 6, 18, 21, 26,
27, 44]. Despite these advances, distillation remains highly
sensitive to conflicts between old and new tasks, a challenge
our method aims to address.
Incremental Object Detection.Compared with class-
incremental learning, incremental object detection (IOD)
is more challenging because the model must preserve spa-
tial localization while coping with missing annotations and
background conflicts,i.e., unlabeled old or future objects
are often treated as background, causing severe supervision
bias. Within two-stage detectors such as Faster R-CNN also
used in our work, ILOD [34] first introduced knowledge
distillation into IOD. Follow-up methods further explored
where and how to distill within the detection pipeline.
Faster ILOD [32], AFD [22], and ERD [11] strengthen
the preservation of old knowledge across the RPN, ROI
features, and the classification and regression heads. To
mitigate non-co-occurrence between old and new classes,
dual-teacher frameworks exploit unlabeled in-the-wild data
and fused teacher probabilities to reduce information asym-
metry [8, 30, 45]. Rehearsal-based methods have also
evolved: Augmented Box Replay (ABR) [25] replays only
foreground boxes using mixup or mosaic augmentation,
avoiding the foreground/background shift caused by full-
image replay. More recently, prototype- and distribution-
alignment techniques have been explored. For example,
Gaussian-mixture modeling of high-dimensional prototypes

helping alleviate semantic drift in the detector head [38].
3. Methodology
3.1. Preliminaries
Problem Formulation.Incremental object detection trains
a detector overNsuccessive stages, each introducing a
fresh subset of categories. Let the full category space be
C={C 1, . . . ,Ct, . . . ,CN }, where each subsetC t repre-
sents a disjoint set of categories introduced at staget, with
the label space progressively growing as new categories are
added at each stage. At staget, the dataD t ={X t,Y t}in-
clude images and annotations. An imagex i ∈ Xt may con-
tain objects from multiple classes inC, but only instances
belonging toC t are annotated. The goal is to update the de-
tector fromM t−1 toM t using onlyD t, without access to
prior datasetsD 1:t−1, while maintaining or improving per-
formance on previously learned classes.
General Object Detector.Following prior work [4,
24, 29, 42], we adopt Faster R-CNN as the base detec-
tion framework, which is trained end-to-end and consists
of three modules: a backbone feature extractorM f , a Re-
gion Proposal Network (RPN)Mrpn, and a region-wise pre-
diction head (ROI head)M roi, with the classification head
denoted byH. Given an input image,M f produces a
dense feature map. On top of these features,Mrpn evaluates
predefined anchors at each spatial location and regresses
bounding-box offsetsl∈R 1×4 to form proposals. The
headM roi then takes both positive and negative proposals
as input and yields final category probabilitiesp∈R |C|+1
(covering|C|foreground classes plus a background class)
with the corresponding bounding box regressionsb.
3.2. Overall Framework
The overall framework of our method is illustrated in Fig. 1,
featuring a two-stage training paradigm that jointly regular-
izes feature learning through cross-head knowledge trans-
fer and preserves classifier-level knowledge via prototype-
based correction.
Specifically, Future-Aware Decoupled Cross-Head Dis-
tillation (FaCHD) decouples the classification head from the
backbone and conducts bidirectional cross-head distillation.
The ROI features from the incrementally trained student
model are decoded by the classification heads of two frozen
teacher models, producing complementary cross-head tar-
gets. The student’s cross-predicted labels are then aligned
with the teachers’ outputs via a distillation loss, effectively
mitigating the conflict between new-class supervision and
old-class distillation. Building upon this decoupled head
design, Region Prototype Semantic Correction (RPSC) fur-
ther compensates for the semantic drift of multi-granularity
region prototypes associated with old classes. By estimat-
ing feature-space misalignment between the old and new
detectors, RPSC recalibrates old-class prototypes and inte-
grates them with current prototypes to retrain the classifi-
cation head, thereby reinforcing knowledge retention at the
classification head level.
3.3. Future-Aware Decoupled Cross-Head Distilla-
tion
IOD requires the model to adapt to newly introduced
categories while preserving discrimination of previously
learned ones. However, the mismatch between new-class
supervision and old-class distillation produces competing
gradients on the shared classifier, leading to biased opti-
mization and accelerated forgetting. To address this issue,
FaCHD decouples the classification head from the back-
bone and introduces a dual-teacher cross-head distillation
scheme that provides stable and consistent guidance across
incremental tasks.
Specifically, the dual-teacher framework is composed of
an old-class expert teacherM t−1 and a new-class interme-
diate teacherM imt .M t−1 provides reliable guidance for
preserving historical knowledge, whileM imt is optimized
only byD t, offering adaptive supervision aligned with new-
class learning. The proposed regions fromM t−1 serve
as the distillation regionsR, ensuring that the knowledge
transfer is concentrated on reliable old-class areas. Follow-
ing the region partitioning and background label reconstruc-
tion strategy proposed in [29], we divide the distillation
regionsRaccording to their Intersection-over-Union (IoU)
with the bounding boxesY t of new-class objects:
R1 ={d j ∈ R | ∀yi ∈ Yt,IoU(d j,y i)≤λ 2 },
R2 ={d j ∈ R | ∀yi ∈ Yt,IoU(d j,y i)> λ2 }, (1)
whereR 1 represents regions that are more likely to belong
to old classes, whileR 2 corresponds to regions that are
more likely to contain new classes.
Then, FaCHD includes a background probability recon-
struction strategy [29] into the teacher model’s target to
construct a future-aware optimization target. Concretely,
for regions inR 1, the background probability from the old-
class teacher is refined through the intermediate teacher,
whereas for regions inR2, the background estimation of the
intermediate teacher is corrected using the old-class teacher:

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Settings
Dataset and evaluation metrics. We conduct experiments
on two widely adopted benchmarks for incremental object
detection: Pascal VOC 2007 [9] and MS COCO 2017 [20].
The Pascal VOC 2007 dataset comprises 9,963 images an-
notated with bounding boxes spanning 20 object categories,
while MS COCO 2017 contains over 118K images with an-
notations for 80 categories.
In the incremental learning setup, we denote a task se-
quence as “A–B”, where the initial stage involvesAclasses
and each subsequent step introducesBnew classes. Fol-
lowing previous studies [4, 24, 29, 31, 41], categories are
partitioned alphabetically to form non-overlapping subsets.
Each image in datasetD t contains at least one instance be-
longing to the corresponding category setC t.
For evaluation, we follow the standard protocol used in
continual object detection. Consistent with prior work [42,
47], we report the mean Average Precision (mAP) com-
puted across Intersection-over-Union (IoU) thresholds from
0.5to0.95, together with AP 75 for COCO-based experi-
ments. For Pascal VOC–based settings, performance is as-
sessed using the AP 50 metric. Notably, our method per-
forms incremental object detection without employing ex-
emplar replay, ensuring a fair comparison with recent ap-
proaches.
Implementation Details.. In line with prior studies, we
develop our incremental object detection model based on
Faster R-CNN [33] using a ResNet-50 backbone [14], ini-

5. Conclusion
In this work, we address the core challenges of incremen-
tal object detection by mitigating the conflict between new-
task supervision and distillation signals in the classification
head, while simultaneously improving knowledge retention.
We introduce Future-Aware Decoupled Cross-Head Distil-

lation, which decouples the student model’s classification
head and regularizes the backbone through targeted distilla-
tion. To further preserve knowledge of previously learned
classes, we propose Region Prototype Semantic Calibra-
tion, a mechanism that estimates semantic drift by compar-
ing feature-map discrepancies across models and compen-
sates for outdated region prototypes. These calibrated pro-
totypes enable effective deduplicated training of the clas-
sification head. In future research, we plan to explore
more flexible prototype modeling that adapts continuously
across tasks, extend our framework to long-tail and open-
vocabulary detection scenarios, and investigate cross-modal
or self-supervised signals to further enhance robustness un-
der large domain or appearance shifts.
